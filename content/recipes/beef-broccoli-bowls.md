ingredients:
  - name: "Rice"
    items:
      - "1/3 cup Calrose rice"
      - "1/2 cup water"
  - name: "Steak"
    items:
      - "6 oz NY strip steak, about 1 inch thick, with the fat cap trimmed to about 1/8 inch"
      - "1/4 tsp kosher salt and a few grinds of black pepper"
      - "1 tsp avocado or canola oil"
  - name: "Broccoli"
    items:
      - "2 cups small broccoli florets"
      - "Pinch of salt"
      - "2 tbsp water"
  - name: "Teriyaki eggs"
    items:
      - "2 large eggs"
      - "1/2 tsp avocado or canola oil"
      - "1 tbsp teriyaki sauce"
  - name: "To finish"
    items:
      - "1/2 ripe avocado"
      - "2 tsp teriyaki sauce, for a light drizzle"
      - "1 tsp toasted sesame seeds"
      - "1 lime wedge (optional)"

steps:
  - title: "Start the rice"
    minutes: 18
    text: "The rice takes the longest, so start it first. Rinse 1/3 cup Calrose rice in a strainer until the water runs mostly clear, then put it in a small pot with 1/2 cup water. Bring it to a boil over high heat, cover, turn the heat to low, and set a timer for 15 minutes. When the timer goes off, turn off the heat and leave the lid on for 10 more minutes."
  - title: "Prep the steak and broccoli"
    minutes: 5
    text: "While the rice simmers, trim the fat cap on 6 oz NY strip steak to about 1/8 inch, pat the steak very dry, and season all sides with 1/4 tsp kosher salt and a few grinds of black pepper. Cut the broccoli into 2 cups of small florets, and leave the steak on the counter until the pan is hot."
  - title: "Heat the pan"
    minutes: 3
    text: "When the rice timer has about 5 minutes left, put a large cast iron or heavy stainless steel skillet over high heat for 3 minutes, until it just starts to smoke."
  - title: "Sear the steak"
    minutes: 7
    text: "Add 1 tsp avocado or canola oil, then the steak. Leave it alone for 3 minutes, flip it, and cook 3 minutes more. When the rice timer goes off during this step, turn off the heat under the rice and leave the lid on. Check the thickest part with an instant-read thermometer. Take it off at 130°F for medium-rare, or cook it about 2 minutes more and take it off at 145°F, the USDA safe minimum. Move it to a cutting board and let it rest for 5 minutes."
  - title: "Char the broccoli"
    minutes: 5
    text: "While the steak rests, turn the same pan down to medium-high. Add the broccoli and a pinch of salt, stand back, and add 2 tbsp water. Cover for 2 minutes to steam, then uncover and leave it alone for 2 to 3 minutes, until the water is gone and the edges are charred."
  - title: "Fry the eggs in teriyaki"
    minutes: 4
    text: "As soon as the broccoli is covered, heat 1/2 tsp avocado or canola oil in a small nonstick skillet over medium heat. Crack in 2 large eggs and cook for 2 to 3 minutes, until the whites are almost set. Pour 1 tbsp teriyaki sauce around the eggs and spoon it over the whites for 30 to 60 seconds, until they are set and glossy and the yolks are still soft. Turn off the heat. For firm yolks, the USDA safe choice, cover the pan for 1 to 2 minutes more."
  - title: "Slice everything"
    minutes: 2
    text: "Slice the rested steak across the grain into 1/4 inch strips, and slice 1/2 ripe avocado. Fluff the rice with a fork."
  - title: "Build the bowl"
    minutes: 1
    text: "Put the rice in a wide bowl, then add the steak, broccoli, and avocado on top in three sections. Slide the eggs into the middle with any sauce left in their pan. Drizzle 2 tsp teriyaki sauce over the steak and broccoli, sprinkle with 1 tsp toasted sesame seeds, and squeeze the lime wedge over everything if you are using it."

tips:
  - title: "Pan"
    text: "Use cast iron or heavy stainless steel for the steak, because high heat damages nonstick pans. Use a small nonstick pan for the eggs, because the sugar in the sauce sticks to bare metal."
  - title: "Teriyaki"
    text: "Thin bottled sauces and thick glazes both work. A thick glaze needs a splash of water in the egg pan so it can coat the whites without burning."
  - title: "No teriyaki on hand"
    text: "Stir together 2 tbsp low-sodium soy sauce and 1 tbsp honey or brown sugar. This makes a little more than one bowl needs."
  - title: "Swaps"
    text: "Ribeye or sirloin can replace the strip steak, and sushi or jasmine rice can replace the Calrose. Frozen broccoli works if you skip the water and cook it a few minutes longer."
  - title: "Lighter version"
    text: "Use 5 oz of steak, 1 egg, and 1/4 avocado for about 825 kcal, 50 g protein, and 36 g fat."
  - title: "Make ahead"
    text: "Cooked rice and sliced steak keep for 3 days in the fridge. Fry the eggs and slice the avocado fresh."

The bowl leans on contrast. The steak has a dark crust and a tender middle, the broccoli gets charred edges, and the avocado and soft yolks turn into a sauce over the rice. Calrose is a medium-grain rice that stays a little sticky, so it holds on to the yolk and the teriyaki.

The teriyaki goes into the egg pan last because its sugar burns quickly. The eggs cook most of the way in a little oil first, and the sauce only needs the final minute to glaze the whites. The drizzle at the end stays light because the eggs already carry plenty of salt and sweetness.

Everything lands together because the steak's rest does double duty. The broccoli cooks in the same pan during those 5 minutes and picks up the browned bits, and the rice finishes steaming on its own at the same time.

Steak, avocado, and yolks make this a rich bowl, so the optional squeeze of lime is worth having. It adds the acid that teriyaki lacks. The steak brings most of the protein, iron, zinc, and B12, the broccoli brings vitamin C, vitamin K, and folate, and the avocado brings potassium and much of the fiber.
